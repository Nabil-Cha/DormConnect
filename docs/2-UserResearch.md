# User Research

---

### A description of the problem the app aims to address

Students are looking for easy ways to network with others and meet new people with similar interests, especially in
communal living especially in communal living environments such as student dormitories. A centralized platform where
students can easily find and join events and activities would help to strengthen social connections.
In addition, a better connection to the neighborhood and a focus on stress management in their studies could promote
student wellbeing and create a supportive community.

---

### Criteria and Assumptions about the users

We think that our users place particular value on intuitive and familiar operation - similar to popular apps such as
Instagram or Tinder. Through simple registration (e.g. via Google or Apple), quick community entry via a QR code and
making it easy to find or create activities, we create a barrier-free entry experience. In addition, gamification
elements such as streaks, haptic feedback and sounds encourage long-term use and increase the fun factor.

---

### Description of the stakeholders and target audience

#### Target demographic:

Our primary target group is students aged between 17 and 30. They are all in a similar life situation - studying at a
university, doing an apprenticeship or at the start of their professional career - and are open to social interaction
with their peers. The target group often moves to a new city and regularly uses digital devices such as smartphones
and computers and is familiar with using social media platforms.

These individuals are interested in getting to know their neighborhood or community better and actively seek out
opportunities to interact with others and engage in shared activities (e.g. cooking together, sports, study groups,
TV nights, chilling out). They are socially motivated and appreciate uncomplicated, low-threshold contact opportunities,
especially when they move to a new city.

#### Stakeholders:

We assume that the most important stakeholders - i.e. residence hall operators, student unions and universities - have
a fundamental interest in promoting the social networking of students. Especially in halls of residence or many feel new
and alone at the start of their studies. Our project aims to use an easily accessible app to make it easier for
neighbors and fellow students to get to know each other.

For operators of student residences, this creates added value: if residents feel comfortable and can network more
easily, satisfaction increases and an active sense of community is created in the residence. Universities also benefit,
as social integration has been proven to contribute to higher motivation and academic success.

We also assume that our solution, thanks to its simple technical implementation - for example via QR codes or a
Google/Apple login function -, can be easily integrated into existing systems. By virtue of the familiar operation and
gamification elements, the use of the app is made attractive for the students, eliminating the need for a long
introduction.

### Hypotheses about user needs or behaviors

***Registration with Google or Apple***

Registering via Google or Apple increases the likelihood that users will successfully complete the sign-up process.

***Justification:***  
Studies show that simple and fast registration processes (e.g., using existing accounts) reduce bounce rates and
improve conversion rates.

***Joining the Community via QR Code***

A QR code makes it easier to join the community and increases the willingness to participate.

***Justification:***  
Observations indicate that users prefer simple and direct access options. A QR code reduces effort and technical barriers.

***Easy Participation and Creation of Activities***

If activities can be created or joined in just a few steps, the usage rate of this feature increases.

***Justification:***  
According to the principle of cognitive ease, people prefer simple processes as they require less mental effort.

***Intuitive Operation Through Familiar Gestures***

Using familiar gestures such as swiping or tapping leads to quicker onboarding and higher interaction frequency.

***Justification:***  
Users transfer familiar interaction patterns from other apps (e.g., Instagram, Tinder), which makes them feel more
confident and motivated.

***Gamification Increases Motivation***

Playful elements such as streaks, vibrations, or sounds increase daily usage and enhance user engagement.

***Justification:***  
Research on gamification shows that small rewards positively influence behavior and foster deeper user involvement.

### Key findings of user interviews

1. Current Event Participation Patterns
    - 3 out of 4 interviewees primarily attend public events organized by their academic departments.
    - These events are typically discovered via word of mouth or through department-run social media accounts (e.g., Instagram).
2. Lack of Community Engagement
    - Interviewees reported feeling less connected to their residential communities than they would like.
    - This reinforces the need for an app that fosters greater involvement and connection among dormitory residents.
3. Participation by Non-Residents
    - Students not living in dormitories often participate in dorm-hosted events.
    - This highlights the importance of allowing users to join communities without scanning a QR code, as QR codes may be too spatially limiting,
4. Relevance of Attendee Information
    - 2 interviewees expressed the desire to see who else is attending an event.
    - This indicates that visible attendee lists could improve user confidence and increase engagement.
5. Demand for Detailed Event Descriptions
    - 2 participants mentioned the importance of clear and detailed event descriptions (e.g., what to bring, whether attendance is limited).
    - Paper prototype testing confirmed that this inforation should be included in an expanded event view.
6. Hosting vs. Attending Behavior
    - The majority of users indicated a preference for attending events rather than hosting them.
    - As a result, the "Create Activity" button should be less prominent in the user interface.
7. User Interface Preferences
    - 3 interviewees expressed interest in differing viewing modes for events:
        1. A list-style view for clarity and overview
        2. A swipe-based interface similar to Tinder, Instagram, or TikTok.
        3. A calendar view to support better scheduling and overview of upcoming events.
8. Additional Feature Requests
    - 2 users requested the ability to export events directly to their personal calendars.
    - 2 interviewees would like a Google Maps integration to easily view event locations.
    - 3 participants suggested adding a polling feature or chat function to coordinate group decisions or event planning.

### Discussion: 2 beneficial user research methods

Two additional user research methods that could complement the semi-structured interviews are **qualitative usability
testing** and **surveys**.

1. **Qualitative Usability Testing (from the TEST stage)**

   This approach centers on watching actual users engage with the application, even if it exists merely as a draft or
   wireframe. Unlike spoken interviews, it unveils practical hiccups: perhaps an icon doesn't convey its function, or the
   navigation feels slightly disorienting. Such issues tend to slip under teh radar unless seen in motion.

#### Why does this fit the project well?

    Our audience is rather familiar with agile, socially attuned platforms like Tinder, TikTok, or Instagram. Any sluggish
    or non-intuitive behavior could quickly repel them. Testing with users allows early spotting of friction, particularly
    in essential flows: joining gatherings, previewing participants, or scanning QR codes to access dorm-based communities.

2. **Surveys (from the LISTEN stage)**

   Surveys enable structured input from a larger audience, often following interviews or usability trials. They work
   especially well to reaffirm patterns, prioritize upcoming features, or get a pulse on what users find necessary.

#### Why does this fit the project well?

    They're practical for checking how students feel about potential functionalities, for instance, is a timeline view more
    welcomed than a card stack? Do attendee names matter? Would they consider hosting a meetup themselves? Given how adept
    this group tends to be with their smartphones, brief and aesthetically clear surveys are likely to receive honest
    responses without much hesitation.

### Persona

#### Leo, the First-Year Student

#### Type

**Student, B.Sc. Computer Science**

#### Bio

Leo has just moved out of his parents' home and started his first semester at university. He lives in a student
dormitory in a new city. Everything is new – the environment, the people, the routines. He is open, friendly, and
curious, but sometimes feels overwhelmed and reserved in direct contact.

#### Goals

- Meet new people
- Gain social confidence in the new city
- Find activities that interest him
- Looking for ways to casually try things out

#### Demographic

- **Gender:** 18 years
- **Status:** Single
- **Location:** Darmstadt, Germany

#### Quote

> "I just want to look around, see who's here and if there are any cool things, without committing right away"

#### Channels

- Smartphone
- Laptop
- Games

#### Habits

- Scrolls for hours daily on TikTok and Snapchat, often in bed at night
- Prefers short, visual content – rarely reads long texts
- Checks his phone right after waking up
- More active in the evenings than in the mornings, as he goes to bed late
- Joins groups passively before engaging actively

#### Motivations

- Spontaneous participation
- Build trust
- Avoid feeling overwhelmed
- Become part of a community

#### Physical/Psychological Traits

- Always has headphones with him
- Usually walking around with smartphone in hand
- Curious and eager to learn
- Has a strong need to belong

#### Frustrations

- He doesn't know anyone and feels isolated
- Fear of embarrassment or not fitting in
- Lack of orientation and impersonal communication

#### Pain Points & Needs

- Doesn't know anyone locally
- Fears embarrassment or exclusion
- Looking for informal, non-committal ways to participate
- Wants clear information, simple procedures, and a welcoming culture
- Dislikes confusing apps or complex processes

### Scenario

Anna is moving into a new dormitory. She needs to join the "Dorm Community App" to see the activities being offered.
Anna scans a QR code, which allows her to join the "Dorm Community" and view the events. She signs up for Tom's barbecue
on Saturday. On the day of the event, the app optionally reminds her and shows her who else is attending nad where the
barbecue is taking place. At the barbecue, Anna quickly meets new people and signs up for more activities in the dorm.

### Reflection

#### What went well?

Finding a name was easy for us - “DormConnect” was quickly found to be a suitable name. The interviews with students
from different faculties also went well and highlighted differences between the personas.

We also managed to define the problem quickly, as we are familiar with similar challenges from our own experience. It
was more difficult to make well-founded assumptions about all potential users, as our team consists exclusively of
computer science students and therefore does not represent the entire target group.

#### What felt like a waste of time?

The entire user research process up until the persona creation was, for the most part, largely familiar to all of us.
We'd had thoughts in the past that such research went into figuring out the target demographic, their characteristics,
and what their needs will be.

However, the team felt like the persona and in particular the scenario felt too generalized. As we were discussing our
potential user base, we'd been thinking about concrete, real-life users with which we may have interacted before in the
real world. The concept of a persona and a scenario, although admittedly necessary for projects whose target demographic
scopes are far wider than ours, felt in our case slightly redundant and more so a hurdle, which may have impacted our
decision-making in design.
